Clockwork Autofill

Hours are written to native Jira worklogs, which Clockwork keeps in sync: they show up in My Work and in timesheets without going through the Clockwork API. Authentication uses your browser session — no tokens, no stored credentials: you just need a tab open and logged in on the site below.

Jira

https:// .atlassian.net

The prefix is enough: Jira Cloud always lives on .atlassian.net, and it is the only host this extension has permissions for. If you paste the full URL it gets cleaned up. Only tabs open on this site are used for the session: another Atlassian site is ignored.

Comma-separated keys. Narrows both the activity search and the issue keys accepted in commit messages.

Commits

Commits are read from Jira itself — the same panel you see at the bottom of every ticket. No Bitbucket tab to keep open, no workspace to configure.

The panel is queried one issue at a time, so the number matters. Besides the issues you touched today, your issues updated in the last three weeks are checked too. Higher means more coverage but a slower analysis. 0 means today's issues only.

Comma-separated. Your Jira name and email are already taken into account: this field is only needed if you sign git commits with a different name.

Working day

Breaks

Breaks do not consume your daily budget: they are holes in the timeline. A block of work that lands on one gets split, and several worklogs reach Jira — the way the day actually looks on the calendar. Add as many as you need.

Recurring meetings

Fixed duration, subtracted from the daily budget before the rest is split across tasks. The ticket is picked in the popup and remembered for the whole week.
